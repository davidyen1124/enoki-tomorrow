Ch.01 — The Farm

Born in the dark.

Most supermarket enoki starts life in a plastic bottle, in a refrigerated room, in near-total darkness. Think wine cellar, but for noodles that are secretly a fungus.

Ch.03 — The Harvest

Pulled. Trimmed. Bagged.

At about 14 cm the whole bunch is plucked in one go. A bottle fruits once; the spent sawdust retires to become compost.

Ch.05 — The Pot

First, a haircut.

Slice 2–3 cm off the base: that woody plug is fused stems and leftover sawdust. Then tear it into small bundles. This is your last chance to make it smaller.

Ch.05 — The Pot

Into the jacuzzi.

Give it a couple of minutes at a proper boil. Never eat enoki raw: it has been behind real listeria outbreaks, and heat is the fix.

Note It will come out looking exactly the same. Remember this.

Why it survives

It's wearing crab armour.

Mushroom cell walls are built from chitin and β-glucans, the same family of stuff as crab shell and beetle wings. You make only a little chitin-cutting enzyme.

And a long, slippery stem you barely chewed gives your stomach almost nothing to grab.

The whole trip, in five facts

What we learned.

  1. 01

    It grows in a fridge.

    Commercial enoki is raised in bottles of sawdust, in the dark, finishing at around 5 °C. Start to harvest: roughly fifty days.

  2. 02

    The look is engineered.

    Wild enoki is short and orange. Darkness, white strains and trapped CO₂ make it pale, long and tiny-capped.

  3. 03

    Always cook it.

    Raw enoki has caused real listeria outbreaks and recalls. A few minutes at a boil sorts that out.

  4. 04

    It's built like a crab.

    Chitin cell walls shrug off stomach acid, and long slippery stems dodge your teeth.

  5. 05

    The reunion is harmless.

    It's insoluble fibre doing fibre things. Chew thoroughly if you'd rather not meet again.
